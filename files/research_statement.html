Huan's Research Statement

Huan Wang, Westlake University, wanghuan@westlake.edu.cn

What I am doing and how I do it

Yes, I am doing "AI research", focusing on the modelling and implementation of intelligence, as an assistant professor at Westlake University. My group specifically focuses on a particular aspect of it, like many others do, called "Efficient AI".

Working as a researcher is an occupation, a means for livelihood, more importantly, also a vocation, a way of "being".

在很多场合下，我们习惯于将自己定义在某个学院某个系某个领域。我将此视作是因为工业分工导致的不得已，而非本应如此。 AI主要被认为是一种科学技术，而科技的目标主要是为了提高生产力。也就是说，为了创造更多的（经济）价值。每个研究者在自过程中获得收入、成就感、reputation等等。但是，AI研究者自己作为人，也要回答，类似人生意义，做AI的深层次意义等问题。我们可以解释，为什么某个技术的motivation是什么，同时也要解释，我们做这个技术的人的motivation是什么（The hidden motivation） -- 而根据我有限的经验，我注意到，这个hidden motivation会决定外化出来的成果等，也会决定技术层面的那个motivation。由此，我对于技术背后的人产生了兴趣。当我觉得这样似乎过于异想天开的时候，我的身旁占有xx等人：the Enlightenment thinkers (e.g., Jean-Jacques Rousseau), modern philosophers (e.g., Immanuel Kant), Ludwig Wittgenstein, and Friedrich Hegel), Zen buddaism, contemporary art (e.g., Marcel Duchamp, Marina Abramović, Deqing Xie), and post-modernism, and of course, the Chinese pragmatism-centered thinkers (e.g., SIMA Tan / SIMA Qian) and their near-modern followers (e.g., QIAN Mu, HU Shih). 我仔细想了下（起码比我思考AI技术层面的论文还要“仔细”），并没有发现他们的逻辑有什么大的问题；与之对应地，我们作为AI研究者自身的矛盾似乎更大。直觉告诉我：有一些东西被隐藏了。我们不是政客、不是商人，是学者，搞清楚怎么回事。

Fundamentally, my professional work is part of all my conscious activities, guided and framed by my perspective on the world and myself. Before I took this position, my thinking as an individual was inspired by a few authentic brains: the Enlightenment thinkers (e.g., Jean-Jacques Rousseau), modern philosophers (e.g., Immanuel Kant), Ludwig Wittgenstein, and Friedrich Hegel), Zen buddaism, contemporary art (e.g., Marcel Duchamp, Marina Abramović, Deqing Xie), and post-modernism, and of course, the Chinese pragmatism-centered thinkers (e.g., SIMA Tan / SIMA Qian) and their near-modern followers (e.g., QIAN Mu, HU Shih). Their ideas and perspectives have inspired me to think of the world as a dynamic, interconnected whole, instead of separate problems for different disciplines / professionals. To a large extent, I am not opting to do so; instead, I had to, after I realized we cannot really "outsource" the very personal problems of our own (e.g., meaning of life, purpose of existence, what kind of job / research / study / family / friendship / relationship we want to have) to some people called "scientists" / "philosophers" / "sociologists" / "psychologists" simply based on the reason that they are more "expert" in these areas than we are.

Admittedly, none of the above appears to have a direct connection to AI or my specific research at Westlake (though they are intrinsically connected at the fundamental level, the way I see it). Yet just like we all exist as an integrated whole instead of a collection of isolated attributes, these ideas and influences profoundly shape how I view current technologies, including AI, how I work at Westlake, how I lead my research group, and what kind of papers we tend to publish, among many others. For this reason, beyond my core research work, I also maintain a long-term focus on philosophy and sociology, not only for the long-term goal of job / occupation / money making as a tool for livelihood, but also for understanding the world and myself as a pure goal in itself. While these are labeled as academic disciplines that seem to stand parallel to computer science and AI — one falling into the humanities and social sciences, the other into science and engineering — I lean toward the view that these disciplines do not stand on an equal footing in parallel, but rather exist in a hierarchical, inclusive relationship. I study them not out of curiosity / hobby, but for a very practical reason: they uncloud the fog for me that science and technology cannot see through (at least in their current capabilities). Hierarchically and ontologically, this pursuit sits at a higher level than my AI research in the technical sense.

What is Efficient AI? What is the long-term mission?

My own work accounts for only a small fraction of the broader field of AI (we all must maintain focused given our limited time and energy). However, at the level of thinking and inquiry, my attention extends far beyond the scope covered in my published papers. Broadly speaking, I focus on two core areas: first, how AI can serve as a better information processing tool (where "better" is defined as stronger performance at a lower cost, grounded in instrumental rationality); and second, AI ethics (grounded in value rationality).

To elaborate on how AI can be a better information processing tool: AI as it exists today is built on computer systems. The almost 80 years of history of AI (since 1948 Winner Cybenetics) coincided with the development of computer systems. What it essentially does is deploy models onto hardware, and the work we do acts as a "bridge" between the two. This bridge is structured in layers: the algorithm layer, the system layer, and the hardware layer. Each layer comes with different potential gains and risks. During my PhD, my work focused primarily on the algorithm layer; now, my research spans both the algorithm and system layers.

We place no restrictions on the upper-layer models and tasks we work on; we pursue cutting-edge topics as they emerge. This is why you will see that our work has covered a wide range of tasks, including classification, style transfer, super-resolution, text-to-image generation, large video models, robot manipulation, and single-exposure imaging, among others. Fundamentally, this is because the processing tool for all these tasks is the neural network model. Our core research objects are inherently models and hardware, and which specific task the model is applied to is ultimately of secondary relevance to our work.
